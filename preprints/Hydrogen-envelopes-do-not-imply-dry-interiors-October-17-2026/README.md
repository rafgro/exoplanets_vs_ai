## Abstract

The inflated radii of young planets can distinguish a light-gas envelope from a pure-steam envelope without establishing whether the underlying planet is water-poor. We quantify this distinction in five benchmarks: TOI-2076 b, c and d, and V1298 Tau c and d. We compare a steam-cooling grid with dry and water-bearing H/He models evaluated through published BICEPS/plaNETic surrogates, and recycle released joint JWST retrieval samples for V1298 Tau c. Under the baseline priors, mass-radius data give water-rich/dry evidence ratios of 0.54-1.61 and broad conditional 95% bulk-water intervals extending to 37-49% by mass. At the central properties of TOI-2076 c, both a nearly dry planet containing 4.0% H/He and a planet containing 30% water plus 9.2% H/He reproduce the radius. Conditioning the V1298 Tau c spectral posterior on its dynamical mass raises the median atmospheric water volume fraction from 1.5% to 9.4% with informed stellar priors. The corresponding fully mixed interior has a median water fraction of 16.3% and a 95% interval of 1.5-40.1%. However, evidence for at least 10% water over a less-than-1%-water alternative is only 1.9:1; flexible stellar priors increase this ratio to 23:1. Steam discrimination is sensitive to structural error and thermal prescriptions. These exploratory, model-conditional results favor H/He in the most inflated benchmarks but do not yield a model-independent bulk-water bound or a population water-rich fraction. Atmospheric-interior partitioning remains an unresolved physical degree of freedom.
