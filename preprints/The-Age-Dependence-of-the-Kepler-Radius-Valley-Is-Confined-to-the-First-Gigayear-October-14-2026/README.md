## Abstract

The number ratio of super-Earths to sub-Neptunes has been reported to rise with host-star age by several groups and is widely read as evidence for Gyr-long core-powered mass loss. Stellar age, however, is entangled with stellar mass, metallicity and transit detectability in every Kepler sample, and isochrone ages carry uncertainties comparable to the intrinsic spread of log age. We re-analyse the super-Earth fraction fSE for 2,007 Kepler planets with 1 < Rp < 4R⊕ (after scaling to the mass- and period-dependent valley) and 1 < P < 100 d around 1,510 hosts, combining the Gaia–Kepler Stellar Properties Catalog with the CKS VII sample, and introduce three controls that have not been applied to this question: a per-star detectability covariate derived from the detected planet’s own multiple-event statistic; a stratified permutation null that shuffles ages only within strata of mass, [Fe/H] and detectability; and an errors-in-variables model that propagates each star’s age posterior into a constraint on the sub-Neptune conversion timescale τ. The raw trend is not monotonic: fSE = 0.31±0.05 for hosts younger than 1 Gyr, 0.48±0.03 at 1–2 Gyr and 0.38–0.44 beyond. The old-age decline in the full Kepler sample is a sensitivity effect, and the apparent rise in CKS is driven by the age–metallicity anticorrelation; the confounders alone predict a young–old difference of the opposite sign (−0.07±0.05 against the observed +0.12, z = 3.7). After nearest-neighbour matching in mass, [Fe/H], period and detectability, hosts younger than 1 Gyr have fSE = 0.31 against 0.47 for matched older hosts (∆ = −0.16, 68% interval [−0.22,−0.11], host-level bootstrap p = 0.007), a deficit present independently in the CKS and non-CKS halves, located at 1.0–1.5R⊕ and mirrored by an excess just above the valley (1.8–2.3R⊕: 24% versus 12%). For hosts older than 2 Gyr the matched difference is −0.02±0.02. The errors-in-variables fit prefers a saturating conversion with τ = 0.4 Gyr (2σ: 0.15–1.5 Gyr; ∆χ 2 = 7.7 against a static population), while continued log-linear evolution improves on a static population by only ∆χ 2 = 2.7. A power analysis shows that a rise of fSE from 0.27 to 0.44 between 0.5 and 8 Gyr would have produced a measured-age slope of +0.66±0.20 against +0.39±0.21 observed. The radius distribution therefore does evolve, but the evolution that current data resolve is largely complete within 1–2 Gyr, and the age trend as measured with Kepler isochrone ages does not discriminate between core-powered mass loss and photoevaporation.
