## Abstract

Which confirmed exoplanets should be prioritized when both environmental plausibility and observational access matter? Individual probabilities of life or civilization are not identified by present data, so a numerical “probability of life” ranking would obscure the dominant uncertainty. We instead construct a reproducible, conditional decision audit. A frozen NASA Exoplanet Archive query contains 6,451 planet records; explicit provenance, status, size and flux checks leave 190 planets for an Earth-adjacent atmospheric screen. Fifteen sourced parameter overrides correct distances, reconcile stellar measurements and preserve dynamical mass information. We propagate 8,192 physical realizations per planet over 128 orbital phases through 648 environmental and instrument scenarios. Eligibility and recoverability are evaluated on the same latent states, and a global minimax calculation examines equal-time portfolios. Proxima Cen b ranks first in 92.4% of scenarios accessible to a southern 39 m telescope. GJ 887 d supplies an alternative when larger temperate worlds are admitted. A corrected distance nearly doubles the nominal angular separation of Gl 725 B c, but its declination excludes it from the southern telescope. A separate injection experiment uses ten published photochemical transmission spectra. At 20 ppm white plus 5 ppm correlated noise, recovery among six inhabited models ranges from 2.99% to 89.36%, despite complete atmospheric-feature recovery in the simulated trials. Omitting airless alternatives yields a 99.53% false-positive rate for a flat spectrum; admitting them reduces that rate to 0.035%. A radio experiment covering 4,792 confirmed-planet hosts shows strong dependence on assumed transmitter power, duty cycle and persistent beaming. The deliverable is a set of auditable priorities and discriminating follow-up questions, not a claim that any world is probably inhabited or that a modeled molecular feature establishes life.
