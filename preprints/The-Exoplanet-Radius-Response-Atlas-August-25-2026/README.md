## Abstract

A Hertzsprung–Russell-like diagram for exoplanets should organize the population into physically interpretable sequences, transitions, and outliers rather than merely maximize a two-variable correlation. We analyzed a 24 August 2026 snapshot of the NASA Exoplanet Archive Planetary Systems table, screening 190 two-parameter projections, 1,365 four-parameter spaces, and 165 radius response surfaces. After precision cuts, the principal sample contains 1,217 transiting planets with measured masses, radii, and incident fluxes; 1,141 also have host metallicities. The best non-tautological organizer is a mass–irradiation control plane in which planet radius is the response field. A collapsed atlas based on mass-corrected irradiation resolves five empirical zones: high-forcing compact planets, moderate-forcing sub-Neptunes, a cool giant shelf, warm compact giants, and hot inflated giants. The underlying response field changes character across a broad transition at approximately 50–100 Earth masses: the radius–mass slope collapses, the irradiation response changes from negative to positive, and the metallicity response changes from a localized positive association at 10–30 Earth masses to a negative association among giants. A two-branch mass–flux–metallicity model reduces host-grouped held-out radius error by 37.6% relative to a single global plane. We state quantitative falsification tests for the transition mass, the two irradiation slopes, the metallicity sign reversal, the sufficiency of the collapsed giant-planet coordinate, and future out-of-sample performance. The atlas is proposed as an empirical population diagram, not as a finalized taxonomy or a causal evolutionary model.
