## Abstract

The ultra-short-period super-Earth 55 Cancri e changes its dayside brightness by more than a factor of ten on timescales of days, and five JWST/NIRCam eclipses in 2022–2023 showed its 4.5µm occultation depth rising from 7±9 to 119 ppm within six days while a cross-correlation analysis of the same frames found strong CO line emission only in the faintest epoch. We re-analyse the published per-visit eclipse spectra (Patel et al. 2024, CDS J/A+A/690/A159) with a diagnostic that has not been applied to them: the contrast between the 4.3µm CO2 ν3 band and the adjacent 3.9–4.2µm window inside each epoch, which cancels the achromatic systematics that make the absolute depths pipeline-dependent and whose sign is the sign of the vertical temperature gradient. The band is in emission in the two faint epochs (+23±6 ppm, 3.8σ) and absent or in absorption in the three bright ones (+1±5 ppm; −12±8 ppm in the brightest), a 38±12 ppm flip within six days that is reproduced by two independent pipelines and survives every band definition and error model we tried. A joint fit of all five epochs with one gas layer of common temperature Tu and CO2-band optical depth τ over a continuum photosphere whose temperature alone varies gives Tu = 2580 K (2300–3600 K), τ ≃ 0.3 (0.1–0.5), χ 2 = 6.3 for 3 degrees of freedom, and is preferred over epoch-by-epoch blackbodies by ∆χ 2 = 26. The same layer predicts the epoch-to-epoch pattern of the CO line strengths (r = 0.85) and, when the underlying deck is dark at 2.1µm, the otherwise impossible inverted colour of Visit 2 (2.1µm undetected while 4.5µm read 1400–1950 K). Tu equals, to within the errors, the radiative-equilibrium temperature of a thin grey layer on the dayside, 2300 K hemisphere-averaged and 2700 K substellar, which is the same whether the surface beneath is a bright 1500 K cloud top or dark 2300 K magma. We conclude that the thermal inversion of 55 Cnc e is not created by the variability: it is the planet’s ordinary radiative skin, always present at 2300–2700 K, and the variability is the continuum photosphere dropping beneath it. Only a condensate deck can lower the 4.5µm photosphere by 1000 K within three orbits; gas cannot, because a ≥ 10 bar CO2 atmosphere neither escapes nor outgasses on that timescale. A delayed magma-ocean/cloud feedback with parameters inside published bounds reproduces the 4.5µm amplitude and the six-day recovery, and with a global-transport lag of ∼17 d the 47.3-day CHEOPS period. We give five falsifiable predictions for the scheduled JWST NIRCam, MIRI and NIRISS epochs.
