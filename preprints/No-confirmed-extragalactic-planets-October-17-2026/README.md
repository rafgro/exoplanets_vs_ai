## Abstract

Eleven claims of planets, or planet-mass populations, beyond the Milky Way have appeared since 1996, together with three claims of planets around Milky Way stars of extragalactic origin. None has been confirmed, and none can be repeated: each rests on a single unrepeatable event or on a statistical inference whose null hypothesis was never computed. We reassess all of them with a common method: reconstruct the published measurement, replace the authors' favourable assumption with the full prior, condition the inference on the fact that the event was found, and compute the null model. For M51-ULS-1b the X-ray dip does not measure the occulter's radius (a grazing star fits within ∆ lnL = 0.7 of a central Saturn), the achromaticity test carries a Bayes factor of only ∼ 2–8, and once detection is conditioned on, the most probable occulter is an M-dwarf tertiary; the event rate also exceeds that expected from bound companions by 102–103. For PA-99-N2 the “6 MJ planet” requires a 0.5 M⊙ lens in the M31 disc, which implies a source size ρ ≃ 0.10–0.20 Einstein radii, whereas the sharpness of the fitted anomaly requires ρ < 0.021; forcing ρ = 0.10 in a finite-source binary-lens refit degrades χ 2 by 65–154 (2% errors), and a rate-weighted lens-population analysis gives a 2–5% probability of a planet in M31. The Fe Kα line-shift statistics behind the “≳ 2000 planets per star” claims in three lensed quasars are reproduced by a static spectrum containing the broad relativistic line that RX J1131−1231 is known to possess, fitted with narrow Gaussians in low-count spectra. The FRB 20180916B planet fails a beaming-independent energy budget by 103.7–107; the published BD+20 2457 two-companion system disintegrates in 4–2500 yr; the NGC 4845 tidal-disruption “super-Jupiter” is one corner of a victim-mass degeneracy spanning 2 MJ to 3 M⊙; the Twin-Quasar and Einstein-Cross “planets” follow from misreadings of event timescales. We identify four recurring failure modes and state the measurements a future confirmation would need.
