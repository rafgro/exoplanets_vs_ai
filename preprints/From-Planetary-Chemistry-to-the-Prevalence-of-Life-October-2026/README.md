## Abstract

The biological factors of the Drake equation describe outcomes without specifying the processes that produce them. We develop a probabilistic framework in which those factors arise from transition rates, finite periods of opportunity, and variation among planets. For a single transition, the probability of completion is 1 − exp(−H), where H is the cumulative hazard. Under a specified model of candidate generation, H is the product of the number of opportunities and the probability that an opportunity establishes the required function. Sequential transitions are described by conditional waiting times rather than products of marginal probabilities; a common replication clock recovers the hard-steps approximation in its rare-event limit. Population averaging connects these transition models to expected counts, while an explicit observation model separates the presence of life from its detection. An illustrative RNA-replicase calibration places substantial prior weight on widespread abiogenesis and is considerably narrower than a diffuse prior on the transition rate. The adopted scenario gives a 0.77 prior probability that at least 10% of candidate worlds become inhabited. This is conditional on the chemical route, its establishment requirements, and the assumed planetary environments, rather than an empirical estimate of the probability that Earth is alone. The framework identifies the measurements needed to improve that estimate: functional frequencies under realistic chemistry, the production and turnover of candidate systems, the probability of sustained replication, and calibrated biosignature surveys. Its contribution is a mechanistic connection between chemistry, evolutionary opportunity, and observable populations, not a choice of logarithmic units.
